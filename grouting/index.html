The most expensive mistake we see on Dunedin sites is pumping grout blind. A contractor shows up, hits running ground in the Leith Valley alluvium, and starts injecting at high pressure with no design logic. The grout travels. It disappears into fractures. The budget evaporates. Dunedin’s geology punishes guesswork. The volcanic basalt of the Otago Peninsula, the loess-covered hills around Maori Hill, and the saturated gravels of South Dunedin all demand completely different injection approaches. Without a proper grouting design that accounts for fracture aperture, groundwater velocity, and confining pressure, you are not sealing anything. You are just filling voids you cannot see. We develop injection programs that match the ground reality, combining field data from test pits with pressure testing before any production grouting begins.

A grouting design without Lugeon testing is just a pump schedule. In Dunedin fractured basalt, we measure what the rock accepts before deciding what to inject.

Methodology applied in Dunedin

Dunedin’s coastal position on the Otago Harbour creates a split personality in the subsurface. Morning fog and persistent easterly swells keep the South Dunedin plain saturated year-round, while the hill suburbs above the cloud line drain freely into jointed schist and basalt. These contrasts mean one grout mix will never work city-wide. In the harbour-fill zone, where the water table sits barely a metre down, we design stable, low-mobility grouts that resist dilution. Up in the Roslyn-Maori Hill belt, where loess collapses on contact with water, we specify slow-penetrating microfine cements to permeate without destroying the soil fabric. CPT soundings give us a continuous profile of permeability boundaries before we ever open a valve. The grouting program is then staged: primary holes for bulk filling, secondary for consolidation, and tertiary verification holes with Lugeon testing to prove the curtain works.

Local geotechnical conditions in Dunedin

The rig setup we specify for Dunedin curtain grouting is always a compact, track-mounted hydraulic unit. Access is everything here. The steep sections of North East Valley and the tight residential lots in St. Clair leave no room for truck-mounted rigs. Our designs call for equipment that can work on a 25-degree slope and still hold a steady 40-bar injection pressure. The real risk is hydrofracture. In the schist zones above the city, a poorly controlled pressure spike can open a fracture into a neighbour’s foundation and suddenly you have grout surfacing in a basement 50 metres away. That is why every design includes a strict pressure-versus-depth envelope and a mandatory refusal criterion. No open-ended pumping. No ‘just keep going until it takes no more.’ The stop condition is defined before the first litre leaves the mixer.

Our services

We deliver grouting designs that match Dunedin’s specific ground conditions, from the saturated harbour-fill to the jointed volcanic rock of the peninsula. Each program is built around three phases of work.

Curtain and Blanket Grouting Design

For dam abutments and deep excavations in fractured Otago schist and basalt. We define hole spacing, stage lengths, and refusal criteria using water pressure testing results. The curtain geometry is verified through oblique check holes.

Compensation and Consolidation Grouting

For settlement control under existing structures in South Dunedin’s soft alluvium. We design fracture-grouting arrays that lift and re-compact soil without uncontrolled surface heave. Real-time monitoring dictates injection sequence.

Pre-Excavation Tunnel Grouting

For headrace tunnels and service trenches running through mixed-face conditions. We specify probe drilling ahead of the face, pre-grout with microfine cement, and verification by core recovery. The goal is a dry, stable face before the roadheader advances.

Common questions

What is the typical cost range for a grouting design in Dunedin?

A geotechnical grouting design for a Dunedin project typically ranges from NZ$2.030 to NZ$7.870, depending on the scope of investigation required, the number of injection phases, and the complexity of the ground model. A small foundation pre-treatment design sits at the lower end. A full dam curtain or tunnel pre-excavation program with multiple verification stages reaches the upper end. Every quote includes the Lugeon testing interpretation and the final injection parameter schedule.

How do you determine the correct grout mix for Dunedin soils?

We start with the grain-size curve of the soil and the aperture of the rock fractures. In South Dunedin sands, a stable bentonite-cement mix with Marsh viscosity around 35–45 seconds works well. In the fractured basalt of the peninsula, we often move to microfine cements with a D95 below 12 microns to penetrate tight joints. The final mix is always confirmed through on-site trial injections with pressure and flow logging.

What verification testing is included in the design?

Every grouting design includes a verification protocol. For rock curtains, this means angled check holes with Lugeon testing to confirm the residual permeability meets the target. In soils, we specify CPTu before and after injection to measure the change in tip resistance and pore pressure response. Core recovery in pre-treated tunnel zones provides direct visual proof of grout penetration.

Can you design grouting for groundwater cut-off in excavations?

Yes, this is a common request for basements and pump stations near the Leith River and Otago Harbour. We design a perimeter grout curtain using a primary-secondary-tertiary hole sequence. The design specifies the target residual permeability, the injection pressure limits to avoid ground heave, and the overlap between adjacent grout bulbs. The result is a measurable reduction in inflow before excavation begins.
